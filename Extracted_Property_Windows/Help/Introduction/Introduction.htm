Introduction

 

SugarsTM for Windows® is a computer program for calculating heat, material and color balances and providing simulations of refining processes for both beet and cane sugar factories to help management with process decisions and operating strategies for process optimization. Sugars is an integrated program using Sugars for process simulation and Visio® for a graphical representation of the flow diagram for the simulation model. The integrated program is completely flexible regarding the type of factory that it can analyze because it uses individual station modules that can be arranged in almost any order to fit the process being simulated.

 

Many complex mathematical relationships are used by Sugars to analyze each flow stream in the model of a sugar factory, or refinery. Mathematical equations are employed to describe the heat of solution and crystallization of sugar crystals, boiling point elevation of syrups, specific heat capacity and density of syrups, sugar crystals, insoluble solids, and gases (e.g.,water and ethanol vapor, CO2, NH3, etc.), solubility of sucrose and supersaturation of massecuites and vapor-liquid-equilibrium of fluid mixtures (e.g., water and ethanol).  Additional equations are used to calculate the latent heat of vaporization, total enthalpy and temperature and pressure relationship for a steam flow (both saturated and superheated). Other algorithms are used to calculate the percent crystallization of a massecuite, steam flow into a heated process stream, centrifugal performance characteristics, color of a flow stream, etc. Balance calculations for the model are solved using iteration techniques until a balance is obtained within a specified accuracy (normally, 0.01%).

 

Calculations completed by Sugars provide a simulation of the various process flows within the model, and the results give a prediction of the steam and water consumption and the quantity and quality of the molasses and sugar production. Results from a simulation are dependent on: (1) the properties of external flows into the process, and (2) the properties (or performance) of each station. Actual factory operating data is used to define the properties of all external flows and the properties (performance values) for each station. Entry windows in Sugars are used to enter all of the properties for the model.

 

Each station processes external flows into the flow diagram to give an output flow (or flows) from the station. Destination stations process the output flows from preceding stations until all of the flows leaving the flow diagram are determined. The program continues iterating until a complete heat, material, and color balance is achieved between the external flows in and flows out of the model. External flows include: beet, cane, or raw sugar into the process; steam; juice from storage; water for dilution; remelt sugar from storage; sweepings; etc. Sugars will calculate the weight quantity of an external flow if it goes to a station that requires a certain quantity to satisfy the specified performance of the station; e.g., steam to boil a pan to produce a massecuite with a specified percent dry substance, or steam to an evaporator station with a specified percent dry substance for the juice leaving the evaporator. Output flows include: sugar, molasses, condensate (water), vapors, fiber, etc. The financial performance of the process is then determined by assigning cost and value numbers to external and output flows. Simply changing the model and displaying the new process revenues will give a quick evaluation of ideas to improve the process.
